## What a chapter needs that a campaign site does not

State of Youth — Digital Inclusion Nepal is a youth-led chapter working on
digital literacy and inclusion across Nepali communities. Two requirements
separate this from a standard advocacy site.

First, **it has to recruit**. A chapter that cannot convert agreement into
membership is a publication, not a movement.

Second, **it has to account for itself**. Partners, funders and evaluators need
to see what the programmes are, what has run, and how the work maps onto the
frameworks they assess against.

## How the platform is structured

**Programmes** — six published areas: digital literacy workshops, school
outreach, future internet awareness, rural technology training, youth innovation
labs, and women in digital skills.

**SDG focus** — the work is mapped explicitly to four UN Sustainable
Development Goals: quality education, decent work and economic growth, industry
and innovation, and reduced inequalities. This is stated as its own section
rather than implied, because it is the vocabulary partner assessment uses.

**Members and events** — a route to join the chapter, and a place for the
events that make membership meaningful.

**Resources** — a library the chapter can publish into over time.

## A note on numbers

The chapter publishes reach figures on its own site. Those are its own reporting
about its own community work, and they belong to the chapter.

They are not outcomes of this engagement and we do not present them as ours.
What we did was design and build the platform; what the chapter has achieved in
communities is the chapter's, and claiming any share of it in a portfolio would
be borrowed credit.

## What we cannot tell you

Whether the site increased membership, and by how much. No baseline was agreed
and no analytics were in place at launch.
