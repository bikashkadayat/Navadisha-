## What the platform had to do

Sikshya Tech Hub runs three things at once: a course catalogue for individual
students, bookable workshops, and technology programmes delivered on school and
college campuses. The commercial problem is that these are bought by different
people for different reasons.

A student wants to know what they will actually build, how long it takes, and
whether prior experience is required. A school principal wants to know who the
tutors are, what happens on their premises, and whether the provider is
credible enough to be let near their students. Those are not variations of one
question.

## How we structured it

The site separates the two routes from the homepage down.

**For students** — courses are browsable by subject across nine areas: AI and
machine learning, prompt engineering, web development, app development, cyber
security, robotics, graphic design, video editing and digital marketing. The
sequence the site describes is deliberately concrete: connect, choose, learn,
build.

**For institutions** — workshops and school partnerships are a separate path,
supported by tutor profiles and team pages. This is the route that carries the
credential burden, because an institution is making a safeguarding and
reputational decision, not just a purchasing one.

The content structure allows courses and workshops to be added without
returning to a developer, which is the same maintainability constraint we apply
to every build of this kind.

## What we would do differently

Nothing about measurement was agreed before launch. We can state what was built
and what is publicly visible, and nothing about what it produced — which is why
this page carries no figures. That is a scoping gap on our side.
